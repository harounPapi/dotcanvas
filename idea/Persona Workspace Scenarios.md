# Persona Workspace Scenarios

This note reframes the persona discussion around the thing that matters most:

`the definition of a project`

The core insight is that .assist should probably not be defined first by job title.

It should be defined by a certain kind of work:

- a bounded project
- a real working corpus
- a meaningful output
- continuity over time
- pressure to be right

If that shape is present, many different kinds of people can fit.

That includes a financial consultant, a student doing a final-year project, a researcher writing a paper, a grant writer, an investor reviewing startups, a market researcher, or an entrepreneur building a business plan.

They are not the same profession.

But they may be doing the same kind of work.

## The Real Question

The right question is not:

`Who is the one perfect job title for .assist?`

The better question is:

`What kind of project creates the need for .assist?`

Once we define that clearly, the personas become much easier to recognize.

## What Is A .assist Project

A .assist project is not just a folder.

It is a living body of work that has:

- `A corpus`: documents, notes, PDFs, spreadsheets, links, transcripts, prior drafts, templates, data, or references
- `A goal`: answer a question, make an assessment, produce a deliverable, or support a decision
- `An evolving understanding`: assumptions, findings, open questions, hypotheses, issues, contradictions
- `A time horizon`: days, weeks, or months, not just one prompt
- `A need for continuity`: the person should not have to reload everything from scratch every time
- `A meaningful output`: paper, memo, deck, proposal, business plan, recommendation, report, summary
- `A trust requirement`: claims need grounding, numbers need traceability, conclusions need support

In one sentence:

`A .assist project is a source-heavy, evolving, outcome-driven body of work that must become a reliable artifact.`

## What Makes Something A Strong Fit

.assist is strongest when the user keeps asking questions like:

- What do I already know?
- What changed since last time?
- What is still unresolved?
- Which source supports this claim?
- What is the latest draft?
- What needs to be delivered next?
- Can you help me turn this material into a real output?

That is very different from generic chat.

Generic chat is good for isolated questions.

.assist should be for sustained project work.

## What The Project Room Should Hold

No matter the persona, the room usually has the same layers:

- `Source material`: the raw corpus
- `Project brief`: what this project is, why it exists, and what success looks like
- `Working memory`: glossary, assumptions, key facts, stakeholders, unresolved questions
- `Analysis`: extracted facts, summaries, comparisons, interpretations, calculations
- `Drafts and outputs`: the things being produced
- `Review state`: comments, gaps, approval status, next actions

This is the important product idea:

The surface personas can vary, but the underlying room is often structurally the same.

## What The Harness Should Do Across All Personas

If these personas really belong in one product, then the harness should help all of them in similar ways:

- recover project context quickly
- turn source material into structure
- preserve assumptions and open questions
- split the work into clear workstreams
- draft real outputs, not just answer questions
- keep claims grounded in sources
- make interrupted work resumable

This is why the shared project shape matters so much.

The role may change.

The harness should not change very much.

## A Simple Definition Of The User

.assist is for people who are trying to turn a project corpus into a trustworthy output.

That is the unifying definition.

Not everyone needs that.

But when they do, the pain is very real.

## Persona 1: Audit Or Financial Consulting

This is still one of the clearest examples.

### Their Project

They have a body of financial, operational, or business material and need to turn it into an assessment, summary, recommendation, or client-facing output.

### Their Corpus

- financial statements
- management accounts
- operating data
- prior decks
- assumptions
- client comments
- supporting research

### Their Output

- audit notes
- findings summary
- management pack
- investment memo
- business assessment
- client presentation

### Why They Fit

This work is evidence-heavy, deadline-heavy, and trust-heavy. The project room is real, and the deliverable matters.

## Persona 2: Student Doing A Final-Year Project

This is important because it shows the product is not only for firms and professional services.

### Their Project

They are trying to move from research material, readings, notes, experiments, and drafts into a final thesis, report, or project submission.

### Their Corpus

- papers
- lecture notes
- datasets
- supervisor comments
- early outlines
- bibliography material
- draft sections

### Their Output

- thesis or PFE report
- literature review
- methodology section
- results write-up
- final presentation

### Why They Fit

The work is long-running, fragmented, and mentally heavy. They often lose continuity, forget what they already read, and struggle to turn scattered material into a coherent final artifact.

## Persona 3: Researcher Writing A Paper

This is similar to the student case, but usually with higher depth and stronger evidence requirements.

### Their Project

They are building a paper from literature, notes, data, arguments, references, and revisions.

### Their Corpus

- papers and citations
- annotated readings
- experiment outputs
- tables and figures
- reviewer comments
- outline versions
- related work notes

### Their Output

- research paper
- literature review
- abstract
- discussion section
- response to reviewers

### Why They Fit

The challenge is not just writing. It is maintaining a reliable map of sources, claims, drafts, and argument structure over time.

## Persona 4: Proposal Or Grant Writer

This is one of the cleanest artifact-first cases.

### Their Project

They need to turn requirements, background material, evidence, and reusable content into a compliant and persuasive submission.

### Their Corpus

- RFP or grant call
- instructions
- prior proposals
- case studies
- organization information
- budgets
- partner inputs
- review comments

### Their Output

- grant proposal
- RFP response
- compliance matrix
- section drafts
- final submission pack

### Why They Fit

The output is explicit, the deadline is fixed, the source material is large, and the penalty for missing something is high.

## Persona 5: Investor Or VC Analyst Reviewing Startups

This is a very strong example of project-based evaluation work.

### Their Project

They are trying to assess whether one startup, or several startups, are worth deeper attention or investment.

### Their Corpus

- pitch decks
- business plans
- financial models
- founder notes
- product materials
- market data
- diligence questions
- call notes

### Their Output

- investment memo
- startup comparison
- risk summary
- market view
- recommendation to proceed or pass

### Why They Fit

This is exactly the kind of work where the person needs to compare sources, preserve judgment history, track open questions, and produce a defensible decision artifact.

## Persona 6: Market Researcher

This persona may exist inside a company, an agency, a consulting team, or as an independent professional.

### Their Project

They are trying to understand a market, category, customer segment, or competitor set and turn that into a useful conclusion.

### Their Corpus

- interview notes
- survey data
- competitor materials
- reports
- desk research
- customer quotes
- trend snapshots
- internal hypotheses

### Their Output

- market landscape
- competitor analysis
- customer insight summary
- demand assessment
- recommendation report

### Why They Fit

The raw material is scattered, the synthesis burden is high, and the value comes from turning fragments into a clear structured view.

## Persona 7: Entrepreneur Writing A Business Plan

This matters because it brings the same project shape into founder workflows.

### Their Project

They are trying to turn an idea, market understanding, assumptions, projections, and strategy into a convincing business plan.

### Their Corpus

- market research
- competitor notes
- product concept documents
- pricing assumptions
- financial projections
- customer interviews
- pitch notes
- advisor feedback

### Their Output

- business plan
- investor narrative
- operating plan
- financial story
- go-to-market summary

### Why They Fit

The founder is often doing synthesis across many incomplete pieces. They need continuity, structure, and help turning rough thinking into a real deliverable.

## What All Of These People Actually Share

These personas look different on the surface, but underneath they all have the same problem:

`They are manually holding a project together in their head.`

More specifically, they all need help with:

- understanding a large corpus
- preserving continuity across time
- extracting what matters
- tracking assumptions and open questions
- comparing versions, options, or entities
- drafting a serious output
- grounding claims in sources
- getting back into the work quickly after interruption

That shared structure matters more than the label on their LinkedIn profile.

## What .assist Should Probably Not Be

This also helps define what .assist is not for.

It is probably not primarily for:

- casual brainstorming with no real project corpus
- one-off questions with no output to maintain
- lightweight chat where continuity does not matter
- tasks with no need for traceability or grounded output

The product gets stronger as the project gets more real.

## The Best Framing

The best framing may be:

`.assist is for project-based knowledge work.`

More specifically:

`.assist helps people turn a messy project corpus into a reliable deliverable.`

That framing is broad enough to include the right users, but still sharp enough to exclude generic chat use cases.

## Project Entry Implication

Because the project is the real object, .assist should support a few natural ways of entering:

- start a new project from a goal
- import an existing folder or bundle
- resume an old or abandoned project

That entry step is part of the product, not just setup.

The system should come back with an understanding of the room:

- what the project seems to be
- what the likely output is
- what material matters most
- what is missing or unresolved
- what should happen next

## Product Consequence

If this is true, then the product should not start by asking:

`What would you like to chat about?`

It should start by understanding:

- what this project is
- what material belongs to it
- what the user is trying to produce
- what the current state is
- what remains unresolved
- what changed since the last session

In other words:

`workspace first, conversation second`

## Working Thesis For Persona Selection

The primary wedge may still be financial consultants, auditors, and adjacent evaluative knowledge workers because they make the project shape especially obvious.

But the deeper truth is larger:

.assist should serve anyone whose work looks like:

- gather material
- understand it
- organize it
- compare it
- draft from it
- revise it
- defend it

That is the real category.

## Working Definition To Carry Forward

Here is the simplest version worth keeping:

`A .assist user is someone working on a bounded project with a real corpus, real stakes, and a real deliverable.`

And the project room exists to make that work feel continuous, grounded, and operational instead of fragile and constantly restarted.
